What it does

The iPad becomes part of the PC.

A small app on the PC captures the screen and encodes it on the graphics card. The iPad app decodes it in hardware and sends every tap, keystroke and Pencil stroke back.

Mirror my screen, or a real second screen

Pick on the PC. Mirror my screen shows your main display on the iPad. Second screen adds the iPad as an extra monitor through a virtual display driver, so windows drag over to it like any other screen. The extra display exists only while the iPad is connected.

Apple Pencil, touch and keyboard

Pressure and tilt reach Windows as Windows Ink, so drawing apps treat the Pencil as a pen. Drawing mode ignores your palm on the canvas. Fingers tap, scroll and drag, a hardware keyboard types, and PC audio plays on the iPad.

The iPad app's settings.

A cable, or Wi-Fi and one code

On Wi-Fi, tap your PC when it appears on the iPad, or scan the QR code the PC shows. The first time, type the PC's six-digit pairing code. After that the iPad remembers it. Over USB there is no code, and plugging in a cable takes over a Wi-Fi session by itself.

Your graphics card does the work

The PC encodes H.264, or HEVC if you turn it on, with NVIDIA, AMD or Intel hardware at 30 to 120 frames per second. On Wi-Fi the iPad asks for lost packets right away, so one drop rarely costs a frame, and the bitrate adapts to your network.

How it works

Three steps, about ten minutes.

One installer on the PC, one app on the iPad. The setup guide has every button name.

  1. Install on your PC

    Run the installer from the download page. It sets up the app and the driver that lets the iPad be a second screen.

  2. Connect the iPad

    Get the iPad app through TestFlight. Tap your PC when it appears, scan the QR code it shows, or type its address. With a USB cable it connects by itself.

  3. Type the code once

    The first time on Wi-Fi, type the six-digit code the PC shows. Then you're on: your screen is mirrored, and Second screen on the PC extends it.

Questions

Before you install

Is it really free?

Yes. EternalMonitor is open source under the MIT license. There is no account, subscription or advertising. If it helps you, a star on GitHub helps us.

Does it work with a Mac?

No. The PC app runs on Windows. On a Mac, Apple's Sidecar already turns an iPad into a second display.

USB or Wi-Fi?

Wi-Fi is the quickest way to start. A cable gives a steadier picture and charges the iPad, and plugging one in takes over a Wi-Fi session on its own. USB needs Apple's free Apple Devices app from the Microsoft Store open on the PC while you stream, and Apple Devices needs Windows 10 version 22H2 (build 19045) or Windows 11. On older Windows 10, use Wi-Fi.

Is the stream encrypted?

Not yet. Pairing stops other devices from connecting, but the picture, sound and input travel over the network as they are. Use it at home or on another network you trust, not on public Wi-Fi. Over a cable nothing goes over the network.

Which iPads and Pencils?

Any iPad on iPadOS 17 or later. Apple Pencil 1st and 2nd generation and Apple Pencil Pro send pressure and tilt. Apple Pencil (USB-C) draws, but has no pressure sensor, so its lines keep one width.

Does it send anything to you?

Both apps share diagnostics with support while they run: device details, errors and stream statistics, including PC names and network addresses. Screen content, audio and pairing codes are never included. You can turn it off in Settings. What is sent, exactly.

Something isn't working

Look up the message you see in the setup guide's troubleshooting tables; each one says what it means and what to do. Problems we already know about, each with a workaround, are on the known issues page. Anything else, tell us.
